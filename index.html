01 / The System

Two Layers. One Edge.

Stan's system has never been taught in a textbook. It emerged from 30 years on the NASDAQ futures desk — and two instruments he trusts above everything else.

LAYER 01 — PRIMARY SIGNAL
S949 Cyclical Momentum Oscillator

Developed by Rob Mitchell — one of the largest S&P futures traders in the 1980s. Stan got it personally. The S949 measures the rate of change in momentum across the market's natural cycle. Its signature: a ±40 oscillator that peaks and troughs in sync with price swings. The core trade is divergence — when price moves one way and momentum moves the other.

±40 Scale Type 2 Divergence NinjaTrader Gann Swing
LAYER 02 — REAL-TIME CONFIRMATION
TickStrike Order Flow Meter

FinancialJuice's TickStrike tells you what the algos are doing right now — in real time, before the candle closes. Visual (green = buying, red = selling, 8-14 scale) and audio (sparse ticks vs machine-gun selling). Stan: "Nothing tells you what's going on right this second except order flow." The oscillator asks where the market is. TickStrike answers what's happening inside it.

11-14 Sweet Spot FinancialJuice Sub-Candle Timing Audio Channel
Signal Interpretation

Three States. Every Second.

Oscillator sets the setup. TickStrike confirms or cancels it.

Confirmed

Oscillator BUY signal + TickStrike green 10-14 in same direction. Highest-probability entry. Both layers agree.

Conflicted

Oscillator gives a signal but TickStrike is in the opposite direction. Wait or size down. Real-time flow is fighting the structure.

Neutral

TickStrike below 9. Market is in low-conviction mode. Neither side is pressing hard. Oscillator signals here are unconfirmed.

03 / AI Training

We haven't trained the AI yet.
Here's why that's the right call.

Before you can teach a machine, you need to understand what it's learning. Phase 1 is capturing exactly how Stan thinks — not just what he does.

OPTION A — PUBLIC / CLOUD
Cloud Model Training

Train using commercial AI (Claude, GPT family). Fastest path to a working model. Broadest capability. Trade-off: training data leaves the local environment — Stan's proprietary edge sent to third-party APIs.

Fastest iteration State-of-the-art models Data sovereignty risk
OPTION B — PRIVATE / LOCAL
Local Model Training

Train using locally-hosted open models (Llama, Mistral via Ollama). Data never leaves our machines. Complete sovereignty over Stan's methodology. Trade-off: slower iteration, more infrastructure.

Full data sovereignty No API dependency Infrastructure investment
Both routes remain open. The training data contains Stan's proprietary edge — the data sovereignty decision matters. Evaluation ongoing.
